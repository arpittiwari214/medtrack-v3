# India DPDP Act 2023 — Operational Compliance for a Small Health/Medication-Tracking App (position as of 22 October 2026)

> **Not legal advice.** This is engineering/product research compiled from public sources. Every substantive date, threshold and exemption below should be confirmed against the Gazette of India text and with Indian counsel before it drives a shipping decision. Several points are secondary-source only and are flagged as such.

## Q1. Current enforcement status as of 2026 — are the Rules notified, when do obligations commence, is the Board operating?

### Takeaway
The DPDP Rules, 2025 were notified on 13 November 2025 with a deliberately staggered commencement: only the Board-constitution machinery is live today, Consent Manager registration (Rule 4) switches on around 13 November 2026 (≈3 weeks from now), and essentially every obligation that touches an app — notice, consent, security, breach reporting, children's data, retention, data-principal rights, cross-border — only commences around 13 May 2027. The Data Protection Board exists on paper (notified 13 November 2025) but as of August 2026 had **no appointed Chairperson and no appointed Members**, so there is no live enforcement body.

### Cited Findings
- The DPDP Rules, 2025 were notified on 13 November 2025, with commencement staggered over 18 months — [Mondaq/law-firm summary via search](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1708164/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-notified); [AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones/)
- **Tranche 1 — in force now:** Rules 1, 2 and 17–21 took effect immediately on notification. These cover commencement machinery, definitions, and the constitution, functioning and operations of the Data Protection Board of India — [AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones/)
- **Tranche 2 — ~13 November 2026:** Rule 4 (registration and obligations of Consent Managers), with corresponding Act provisions Sections 6(9) and 27(1)(d) — [AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones/); [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/)
- **Tranche 3 — ~13 May 2027:** Rules 3, 5–16, 22 and 23 — i.e. manner of notice, reasonable security safeguards, breach notification, retention/erasure, verifiable parental consent and children's exemptions, SDF obligations, cross-border transfer, exercise of data-principal rights, appeals to the Appellate Tribunal, and the government's power to call for information. Section 43A of the IT Act is repealed on the same date — [AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones/); [AMSS (Shardul Amarchand)](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/)
- **Date discrepancy in sources:** AZB gives 12 November 2026 and 12 May 2027; Shardul Amarchand's annexure gives 14 November 2025 / 14 November 2026 / 14 May 2027; other summaries give 13 November 2026 / 13 May 2027, counting from a 13 November 2025 publication. The spread is ±1–2 days and turns on the Gazette publication date — [AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones/); [AMSS](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/); [search synthesis of multiple firm summaries](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/)
- **Board legally established:** notified 13 November 2025 by G.S.R. 844(E) under s.18(1) and (3), head office in the National Capital Region; G.S.R. 845(E) provides for four Members — [search synthesis; see also](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones/)
- **Board not staffed:** On 6 May 2026 MeitY issued a circular and public advertisement inviting applications for one Chairperson and four Members to form a panel for a Search-cum-Selection Committee — [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/govt-establishes-data-protection-board-under-dpdp-act-154594.html)
- A LiveLaw analysis dated 1 August 2026 states that as of that month the Board has **no appointed Chairperson and no appointed Members** — [LiveLaw, "India's Data Protection Board: Established In Law, Absent In Fact"](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/india-data-protection-board-established-law-543751)
- **Pending acceleration:** At a stakeholder meeting on 22 January 2026 MeitY proposed cutting the data-fiduciary compliance runway from 18 months to 12 months (which would move the main deadline from ~13 May 2027 to ~13 November 2026), bringing Rules 15 and 23 into effect immediately, operationalising Rule 8(3) within three months, moving Rule 13 (SDF obligations) to 12 months, and bringing s.10(1) SDF notification forward to the date of the amendment. Industry feedback was sought by 4 February 2026 — [S.S. Rana & Co.](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs/); [Takshashila](https://takshashila.org.in/content/blogs/20260126-meity-considers-reducing-dpdpa-compliance-timelines.html); [Mondaq, 15 April 2026 — still described as "proposed"](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1773554/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs)
- **No amendment notified as at October 2026:** searches found no Gazette amendment to Rule 1 shortening the runway; one commentary notes the Government confirmed the November 2026 and May 2027 deadlines in a written Lok Sabha answer on 12 August 2026 — [theprivacylabs.com](https://theprivacylabs.com/blogs/dpdp-13-november-2026-what-starts); [indiadatalaw.org](https://indiadatalaw.org/deadlines/)
- **Unsettled:** sources disagree on whether the Board can inquire and levy penalties from 13 November 2026 or only from May 2027 — [search synthesis across](https://theprivacylabs.com/blogs/dpdp-13-november-2026-what-starts) and [consentos.in](https://consentos.in/learn/dpdp-compliance-timeline/)

### Inferences
- **Nothing in the DPDP Rules legally binds a medication-tracking app's product behaviour today.** The operative compliance date for an ordinary (non-Consent-Manager, non-SDF) app is ~13 May 2027 — about 7 months away as of this writing. Treat everything in Q2/Q4 below as "build now, mandatory May 2027".
- The MeitY 12-month proposal is a live tail risk: if notified, the main deadline collapses to ~13 November 2026, i.e. effectively immediately. Build to the May 2027 spec but do not schedule the work *for* May 2027 — a notified amendment would leave no runway.
- Because the Board is unstaffed, there is currently no body able to receive a breach report, register a Consent Manager, or impose a penalty. Practically this means (a) near-zero enforcement risk in the short term, and (b) a likely enforcement backlog/burst once members are appointed. It is not a reason to skip the build.
- The old regime has not been repealed yet: **IT Act s.43A and the SPDI Rules, 2011 remain in force until the May 2027 tranche**, and the SPDI Rules *do* treat health/medical records as sensitive personal data. So a health app today is governed by the older, stricter-on-paper SPDI regime, not by DPDP. (Repeal of s.43A is in the May 2027 tranche per [AMSS](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/); the SPDI treatment of health data is general knowledge and is listed as a gap below.)

### Gaps
- Could not verify the exact Gazette publication date (13 vs 14 November 2025) from a primary source; the ±1 day affects every downstream date.
- No primary-source confirmation of whether the MeitY 12-month amendment has been gazetted between April and October 2026. Must be checked on meity.gov.in / egazette.
- No source found confirming current (October 2026) Board membership status; the most recent evidence is the LiveLaw piece of 1 August 2026.
- Did not find a citable source for the continued operation of the SPDI Rules, 2011 and their treatment of health data as "sensitive personal information" in the interim period. This should be confirmed — it is the regime that actually binds the app *today*.

## Q2. What exactly must a Data Fiduciary do — notice, consent, withdrawal, grievance, DPO, breach

### Takeaway
Rule 3 fixes notice contents (itemised data, specified purposes, specific description of the goods/services, and a working link to withdraw consent / exercise rights / complain to the Board), Rule 6 fixes minimum security measures including encryption and a one-year log retention floor, Rule 7 imposes a two-tier breach report with **no materiality threshold at all**, Rule 8 requires erasure on purpose-exhaustion or consent withdrawal, and Rule 14 requires published grievance channels with a hard 90-day outer limit. A DPO is only mandatory for Significant Data Fiduciaries; everyone else must publish a contact point.

### Cited Findings
**Notice (Rule 3 / s.5)**
- The notice must be given before collecting personal data, must stand alone and cannot be bundled with service agreements or other documents, must be in plain language, and must be understandable to its audience (for children, in language a child can follow) — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/)
- Minimum contents: an **itemised** description of the personal data; the specified purposes; a specific description of the goods/services provided or uses enabled; and a communication link for withdrawing consent, exercising rights, and complaining to the Board — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/)
- The notice must also explain how Data Principals exercise their rights — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/)

**Consent and withdrawal (s.6 / Rule 3(c)(i))**
- Consent must be "free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous with a clear affirmative action" (s.6) — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/)
- Under **Rule 3(c)(i), withdrawing consent must be about as easy as giving it** — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/)
- "Consent may be withdrawn at any point in time." Consent is not required for enumerated "legitimate uses", which include **medical emergencies** and voluntary sharing for a specified purpose — [PRS Legislative Research](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/digital-personal-data-protection-bill-2023)
- The Central Government may exempt notified fiduciaries, **including startups**, from the notice requirement, having regard to volume and nature of data — [PRS Legislative Research](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/digital-personal-data-protection-bill-2023)

**Security safeguards (Rule 6 / s.8(5))**
- Required measures: encryption, obfuscation, masking or virtual tokens; access controls; logs, monitoring and review; backups and resilience; and security provisions in the fiduciary–processor contract — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/)
- Also listed: pseudonymisation or anonymisation, maintaining confidentiality/integrity/availability; **processors must meet the same safeguard standard as fiduciaries** — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/)
- Some minimum technical safeguards are qualified as applying "wherever applicable"; contractual security terms with processors apply "where applicable" — [AMSS](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/)
- **Logs and personal data must be retained for at least one year** — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/); [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/)

**Breach notification (Rule 7 / s.8(6))**
- **To affected Data Principals:** notify each affected person "without delay", via their account or registered contact. Content: nature/extent/timing of the breach, likely consequences, mitigation steps taken, protective steps the individual can take, and a business contact. The breach *location* need not be disclosed to data principals — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/); [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/); [AMSS](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/)
- **To the Board, tier 1:** inform without delay, with nature, extent, timing, location and likely impact — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/)
- **To the Board, tier 2:** a detailed report **within 72 hours** (or longer if the Board permits) covering the facts, mitigation, findings as to the cause, remedial measures, and a report on the notifications sent to data principals — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/); [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/)
- **There is no materiality threshold:** reporting to the Board and notice to all affected data principals is required "regardless of the impact or materiality of a PD breach" — [AMSS](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/)

**Retention and erasure (Rule 8 / s.8(8))**
- Erase personal data once the specified purpose is no longer served **or consent is withdrawn**, unless retention is required by law — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/); [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/)
- Traffic data and processing logs: minimum one year from processing, under Rule 8(3) — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/); the Seventh Schedule purposes drive this and it was **not** in the draft rules — [AMSS](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/)
- The 3-year inactivity-erasure rule and the 48-hour pre-erasure warning apply only to **Third Schedule** classes — large e-commerce, online gaming and social media intermediaries above user thresholds — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/); [AMSS](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/)

**Rights, grievance and DPO (Rule 14)**
- Fiduciaries and Consent Managers must **publish** how to make a rights request, including any identifiers required, and requests must go through those published channels. Data Principals may nominate another person to exercise their rights — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/)
- Grievance mechanisms must be published on the website or app; grievances must be resolved within a reasonable period and **no later than 90 days** — [AMSS](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/); [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/)
- Business contact details of the **DPO (if applicable)** — or of a person able to answer questions about processing — must be prominently published on the website or app — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/)
- A DPO **based in India**, reporting to the Board of Directors, is an **SDF-only** obligation under Rule 13 — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/)
- Rights of data principals: information about processing, correction, erasure, nomination, and grievance redressal. Data-principal **duties** include not filing false or frivolous complaints and not impersonating — breach carries a penalty up to ₹10,000 — [PRS](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/digital-personal-data-protection-bill-2023)
- General fiduciary duties under the Act: reasonable effort to keep data accurate and complete; reasonable security safeguards; breach notification; erasure on purpose-exhaustion — [PRS](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/digital-personal-data-protection-bill-2023)

### Inferences
**Engineering checklist — LEGALLY MANDATORY by ~May 2027 (build now):**
1. A standalone, non-bundled notice screen that is not part of the T&Cs or privacy policy, rendered **before** first collection. It needs: an itemised data inventory (not "we collect usage data" — list the fields: name, DOB, medication names, dosages, adherence timestamps, reminder times, device token…), the purpose per item, a description of the app's function, and three live links: withdraw consent, exercise rights, complain to the Board.
2. A consent record store: per-purpose consent state, timestamp, notice version shown, and language shown. Required to evidence "specific, informed, unconditional".
3. A **withdraw-consent** control reachable in the same number of taps as the consent flow (Rule 3(c)(i) is a literal UI requirement, and the easiest one to fail an audit on).
4. Withdrawal must trigger erasure (Rule 8) — so build erasure as a real, testable job, not a soft-delete flag. A soft-delete that keeps the row is not erasure.
5. Encryption at rest and in transit, access control, and audit logging — logs retained **≥1 year** (Rule 6 / Rule 8(3)). Note the tension: the erasure duty and the 1-year log-retention floor pull in opposite directions; keep logs that are about *processing events* separate from the personal-data store so you can erase the latter and retain the former.
6. A breach runbook with a **72-hour** clock to the Board and "without delay" to every affected user, with **no materiality filter**. For a small app this is the highest-effort, lowest-expected-frequency item: pre-write the user notification template (nature, consequences, mitigation, user actions, contact) and the Board report template now, because 72 hours is not enough time to draft them.
7. A published grievance channel with an SLA tracker; the hard stop is 90 days but that is an outer limit, not a target.
8. A published contact point for processing questions. A full DPO is *not* required unless notified as an SDF.
9. Data processor contracts (cloud, analytics, SMS/push, crash reporting) carrying the same security obligations — Rule 6 makes the processor's standard your problem.

**Good practice, NOT mandatory:** DPIAs (SDF-only), appointing a formal DPO (SDF-only), the 3-year inactivity purge and 48-hour pre-erasure warning (Third Schedule classes only — a medication app is not an e-commerce/social-media/gaming intermediary), ISO 27001 certification, data-minimisation beyond stated purposes.

### Gaps
- The exact Rule 3 text on *how* notice must be presented (modal, link, scroll-to-accept) is not in the secondary sources; the Gazette text is needed.
- No source gave a statutory response deadline for *rights* requests (as opposed to the 90-day grievance limit). Conventus Law expressly says the article gives no response deadline.
- Whether the "startup exemption" from notice under s.17(3)/(5) has been operationalised by notification — no source found. For a small app this could matter a great deal; it should be checked directly.

## Q3. Is health data a special/sensitive category under DPDP? How does this differ from GDPR Art. 9?

### Takeaway
No. DPDP deliberately dropped the "sensitive personal data" category that earlier Indian drafts had; health data is ordinary "personal data" with exactly the same baseline obligations as an email address. GDPR Art. 9 by contrast prohibits health-data processing unless a second, separate condition is met. The practical effect is that DPDP imposes *no extra legal step* for health data, while leaving the same data exposed to a uniform (and arguably under-calibrated) standard — and the gap is absorbed instead by sector rules (ABDM/EHR/telemedicine) and by contract.

### Cited Findings
- The DPDP Act folds health information into general "personal data" and does not create a separate sensitive or special category; earlier drafts such as the 2019 Bill did define "sensitive personal data" but the enacted Act dropped it — [search synthesis; see](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/02537176251370651) and [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/cross-border-transfer-healthcare-data-reconciling-india-dpdpa-gdpr-542388)
- Health data is "subsumed under the term 'personal data'" (s.2(t)); the author argues a basic email address and an oncology report get the same baseline treatment — [LiveLaw, "Cross-Border Transfer Of Healthcare Data: Reconciling India's DPDPA With GDPR"](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/cross-border-transfer-healthcare-data-reconciling-india-dpdpa-gdpr-542388) *(this is one author's argument, not a settled finding)*
- Under GDPR, special-category processing (which includes data concerning health, genetic data, biometric data, sex life and sexual orientation) is **prohibited unless a second Art. 9 condition or an exemption applies** — [Jentis explainer on GDPR Art. 9](https://www.jentis.com/blog/what-is-article-9-of-the-gdpr-sensitive-data-explained-and-how-companies-remain-compliant)
- DPDP applies only to **digital** personal data, whereas GDPR applies to personal data whether or not digital — [Latham & Watkins, DPDPA vs GDPR](https://lw.com/en/insights/2023/12/Indias-Digital-Personal-Data-Protection-Act-2023-vs-the-GDPR-A-Comparison)
- The uniform standard means a medical breach is not distinguished from a routine credential leak; the LiveLaw author cites a 2026 ransomware attack on a Kerala hospital (800 GB) to make the point — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/cross-border-transfer-healthcare-data-reconciling-india-dpdpa-gdpr-542388)

### Inferences
- **Practical difference #1 (legal process):** Under GDPR you need an Art. 6 basis *plus* an Art. 9 condition (for a consumer health app, almost always explicit consent). Under DPDP you need consent only. There is no second gate, no "explicit consent" tier, and no mandatory DPIA trigger for health data.
- **Practical difference #2 (risk calibration):** The penalty ceiling for a security failure (₹250 crore) does not scale up for health data, nor down for a small app — but the Board sets the actual amount case-by-case on gravity, and the sensitivity of health data will land in that discretion. So health sensitivity re-enters at the *quantum* stage, not the *obligation* stage.
- **Practical difference #3 (product):** Because DPDP does not require a separate tier for health data, the honest engineering position is that DPDP is a *floor*, not a ceiling, for a medication app. Treating medication data as if it were Art. 9 special-category data (explicit, separately-captured consent; strong encryption; strict access logging; no secondary use; no advertising/profiling use) is **good practice, not legally mandatory** — but it is also what makes the app portable to GDPR markets later and what the sector rules (Q6) effectively assume.
- **Carry-forward risk:** If the app ever serves EU/UK users, Art. 9 bites independently and the DPDP-minimal consent flow will not satisfy it. Designing one consent flow to the stricter standard is cheaper than two.

### Gaps
- No source found confirming whether MeitY has used any power to designate health data for special treatment by notification (e.g. via SDF designation of health platforms, or a Rule 13(4) localisation order covering health data). See Q6.
- The interim position under the SPDI Rules 2011 (which *do* classify medical records and health condition as sensitive personal data and require stricter consent) was not sourced in this research and should be verified — it is the operative rule until ~May 2027.

## Q4. Languages, itemised consent, children under 18 / verifiable parental consent, SDF thresholds

### Takeaway
Notice and the consent request must be accessible in English **or any of the 22 Eighth Schedule languages** at the user's option, which is a build requirement not a translation afterthought. Consent must be itemised per data item and purpose. The hardest item for a health app is children: a "child" is anyone **under 18**, verifiable parental consent is required, and the Fourth Schedule exemption is **entity-based** — it reaches clinical establishments and healthcare professionals, not consumer apps, so a general-public medication app almost certainly cannot rely on it. SDF criteria are discretionary under s.10 and **no thresholds have been notified**.

### Cited Findings
**Languages**
- The Act gives the Data Principal the option to access the notice in English or any language specified in the **Eighth Schedule to the Constitution** (22 languages). Sources disagree on placement: several put the language option at **s.5(3)** (notice) and several at **s.6(3)** (consent request); the most consistent reading is that it appears in both — [search synthesis across](https://myctrl.tools/frameworks/india-dpdpa/5-3), [King Stubb & Kasiva](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/notice-obligations-under-dpdp-act-2023-clarity-access/), [Certinal](https://www.certinal.com/blog/multilingual-consent-under-dpdp-act)
- Rule 3 adds a presentation standard: notices must be easily understandable, **separate from other information**, and in plain language; one commentary notes the language option must be available **at the moment of the request**, so it must be built into the product rather than handled as a later translation task — [Sansa Legal on drafting a Rule 3 notice](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/how-to-draft-a-dpdp-notice-that-meets-rule-3-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025); [Certinal](https://www.certinal.com/blog/multilingual-consent-under-dpdp-act)

**Itemised consent**
- Rule 3 requires an **itemised** description of the personal data sought and the specified purposes; consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous with a clear affirmative action (s.6) — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/); [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/)

**Children**
- A child is **anyone under 18** — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/); [PRS](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/digital-personal-data-protection-bill-2023)
- Verifiable consent of a parent/lawful guardian is required before processing (s.9(1)); the fiduciary must confirm the parent is an **identifiable adult**, using identity and age details it already lawfully holds, or details provided voluntarily via a **virtual token** issued by an authorised entity — the Rules expressly allow **Digital Locker Service Providers** — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/); [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/); [AMSS](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/)
- Platforms must observe due diligence to ensure the person giving verifiable parental consent is an adult — [Tsaaro](https://tsaaro.com/blogs/safeguarding-minors-online-understanding-parental-consent-obligations-and-behavioural-monitoring-restrictions-under-the-dpdpa-and-dpdp-rules)
- PRS notes the under-18 rule "may require age verification of **all** users" — [PRS](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/digital-personal-data-protection-bill-2023)
- Tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising directed at children are prohibited; processing detrimental to a child's well-being is prohibited, and "detrimental" is **not defined** — [PRS](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/digital-personal-data-protection-bill-2023); [AMSS](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/)
- **Fourth Schedule, Part A (entity-based exemptions):** clinical establishments, mental health establishments, and healthcare/allied healthcare professionals are exempt from verifiable parental consent and the related prohibitions, but **only to the extent necessary for the protection of the child's health**. Educational institutions may track only for educational activity or safety; school-transport providers may track location only for safety — [S.S. Rana, "Ensuring Child Safety under the DPDPA"](https://ssrana.in/articles/ensuring-child-safety-under-the-dpdpa/); [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/)
- **Fourth Schedule, Part B (purpose-based exemptions):** government subsidy/benefit processing, **real-time location tracking of a child for safety** (a new exempt purpose added in the final Rules), and processing needed to confirm the user is not a child — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/); [AMSS](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/); [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/11/223-dpdp-rules-real-time-child-tracking-without-consent/)
- Exemptions apply **only** where the Rules list the entity or purpose and the stated conditions are met; an app is not covered merely because it is health-related, and a general wellness/fitness/consumer app would likely still need verifiable parental consent — [search synthesis of](https://ssrana.in/articles/ensuring-child-safety-under-the-dpdpa/) and [Tsaaro](https://tsaaro.com/blogs/safeguarding-minors-online-understanding-parental-consent-obligations-and-behavioural-monitoring-restrictions-under-the-dpdpa-and-dpdp-rules)
- Persons with disabilities: lawful guardians must give verifiable consent under a separate rule; the definition covers autism, cerebral palsy, intellectual disability or multiple severe disabilities where the person cannot make legally binding decisions even with support — [AMSS](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/)

**Significant Data Fiduciaries**
- SDF status arises only on **notification by the Central Government** under s.10(1); the ₹150 crore penalty band applies only to notified entities — [Konfirmity](https://www.konfirmity.com/blog/dpdp-penalties)
- Rule 13 obligations once notified: annual DPIA **and** annual audit with significant observations reported to the Board; an **India-based DPO reporting to the Board of Directors**; an independent data auditor; due diligence that algorithmic software does not pose risks to data principals' rights; and under **Rule 13(4)** specified personal data and associated traffic data must not be transferred outside India — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/); [AMSS](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/)
- The categories of data subject to the Rule 13(4) localisation ban are to be identified by the Central Government on the recommendation of a committee including MeitY officials — [AMSS](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/)
- Neither Conventus Law nor AMSS describes any numeric criteria for SDF designation; Conventus expressly states the article does not describe the designation criteria — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/)

### Inferences
**MANDATORY (by ~May 2027):**
- **Language:** the notice/consent screen must offer English + Eighth Schedule languages *at the point of display*. This is a product architecture decision today: externalise every notice/consent string, version them, and record which language version a user consented under. For a medication app, translating only the marketing site is not compliance. (Practical scoping: the duty is to give the *option* to access in a Schedule language; whether all 22 must be pre-translated or provided on request is not resolved by the sources — see Gaps.)
- **Itemised consent:** one consent record per (data item × purpose). Specifically for a medication app, the following are *different* purposes and should not be bundled into one checkbox: (a) storing the medication schedule to deliver reminders, (b) storing adherence history, (c) sharing with a caregiver/family member, (d) sharing with a clinician, (e) analytics/crash reporting, (f) any research or aggregate use, (g) any marketing. Bundling (a) with (f) is the classic failure mode and is squarely a "free, specific, unconditional" violation.
- **Children:** the Fourth Schedule Part A exemption is for **clinical establishments and healthcare professionals**, not for app publishers. A consumer medication app operated by a software company is very unlikely to qualify. So the app must either (i) implement verifiable parental consent for under-18s, or (ii) deliberately exclude under-18s and implement an age gate that is more than a self-declared checkbox. Note Part B expressly exempts "processing needed to confirm the user is not a child", which is the legal hook for running the age check itself.
- Verifiable parental consent in practice means DigiLocker-style virtual-token verification of the parent as an identifiable adult. For a small app this is a real integration, not a dialog box. It is the single largest engineering item in this checklist.
- **No behavioural tracking, profiling or targeted advertising on any user known or reasonably suspected to be a child.** For a medication app this should be a global product rule, because the adherence dataset is exactly the kind of behavioural monitoring the provision targets.

**Product recommendation:** the cheapest compliant posture for v1 is **18+ only**, enforced at signup, with the under-18 case handled as "a parent/guardian creates the account and manages the child's medication as a care recipient" — but note that this does not dodge s.9 if the *data subject* is the child. A guardian-managed child profile still processes a child's personal data and still needs verifiable parental consent; the saving grace is that the guardian is present and identifiable, which makes the verification tractable. This needs legal confirmation.

**NOT mandatory for a small app:** all Rule 13 SDF obligations (DPIA, annual audit, India-resident DPO, algorithmic due diligence, Rule 13(4) localisation) — unless and until individually notified. A small medication app is not plausibly an SDF today.

### Gaps
- **No SDF thresholds exist to report.** s.10(1) lists discretionary factors (volume and sensitivity of data, risk to rights, risk to electoral democracy, security of the State, public order) and designation is by notification; no numeric threshold has been notified and no source found any list of notified SDFs. If a source claims a specific user-count threshold for SDF status, it is wrong.
- Whether all 22 Eighth Schedule languages must be pre-translated, or whether on-request provision suffices, is not resolved by any source found.
- Which "authorised entities" besides Digital Locker Service Providers can issue the parental-verification virtual token, and whether a non-DigiLocker method (e.g. a verified payment-instrument check) is acceptable, is not addressed in the sources.
- No source addressed the specific case of a guardian-managed child profile in a consumer health app — i.e. whether the Part A healthcare exemption can ever be read to cover a software provider acting on a clinician's instruction. This is the key open legal question for this product.

## Q5. Penalty amounts

### Takeaway
Every figure is a statutory **ceiling** ("may extend to"), imposed by the Board after inquiry and set case-by-case; the headline numbers are ₹250 crore for a security-safeguard failure, ₹200 crore each for breach-notification failure and children's-data failure, ₹150 crore for SDF obligations, ₹50 crore residual for anything else, and ₹10,000 against a data principal.

### Cited Findings
| Provision breached | Ceiling | Source |
|---|---|---|
| s.8(5) — failure to take reasonable security safeguards to prevent a breach | **₹250 crore** | [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/); [PRS](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/digital-personal-data-protection-bill-2023); [dpdpa.com Schedule](https://dpdpa.com/theschedule.html) |
| s.8(6) — failure to notify the Board or affected Data Principals of a breach | **₹200 crore** | [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/); [Konfirmity](https://www.konfirmity.com/blog/dpdp-penalties) |
| s.9 — breach of additional obligations for children | **₹200 crore** | [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/); [PRS](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/digital-personal-data-protection-bill-2023) |
| s.10 — breach of additional obligations of a Significant Data Fiduciary | **₹150 crore** | [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/); [Konfirmity](https://www.konfirmity.com/blog/dpdp-penalties) |
| Breach of any other provision of the Act or Rules (residual) | **₹50 crore** | [dpdpa.com Schedule](https://dpdpa.com/theschedule.html) |
| Breach of a Data Principal's duties (false/frivolous complaints, impersonation) | **₹10,000** | [PRS](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/digital-personal-data-protection-bill-2023) |

- Each entry is a maximum, not a fixed fine; the Board sets the amount case by case — [Konfirmity](https://www.konfirmity.com/blog/dpdp-penalties); [miniOrange](https://www.miniorange.com/blog/dpdp-act-non-compliance-penalties/)
- Factors the Board weighs: nature, gravity and duration of the breach; the type of personal data affected; repetitive character; gains realised or losses avoided; and mitigation efforts — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/)
- The s.10 (₹150 crore) band applies only to entities the government has **notified** as Significant Data Fiduciaries — [Konfirmity](https://www.konfirmity.com/blog/dpdp-penalties)
- Penalties are imposed by the Board after inquiry; appeals lie to **TDSAT** — [PRS](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/digital-personal-data-protection-bill-2023); [miniOrange](https://www.miniorange.com/blog/dpdp-act-non-compliance-penalties/)
- There is also a voluntary-undertaking route: the Board can accept voluntary undertakings as an alternative to proceedings — [AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones/)

### Inferences
- The two bands most likely to bite a small medication app are **₹250 crore (security)** and **₹200 crore (breach notification)**. Notably, failing to *report* a breach is penalised almost as heavily as causing one — and because Rule 7 has no materiality threshold, the reporting failure is far easier to commit accidentally (e.g. a minor exposure judged "not worth reporting"). The engineering conclusion: **report everything; never make a materiality judgement call.**
- The ₹200 crore children's band means the age-gate/parental-consent work in Q4 is not a cosmetic item.
- The ceilings are not scaled to company size; mitigation and cooperation are the only levers a small operator has, which argues for documented evidence of the compliance programme (consent logs, notice versions, breach runbook, DPIA even though not required) as insurance in the quantum assessment.

### Gaps
- No source found giving the exact wording/entry count of the Schedule beyond these bands; the dpdpa.com page is a mirror, not the Gazette. Verify against the official Act PDF on meity.gov.in.
- No source gave an entry for failure to give notice under s.5 specifically — it presumably falls in the ₹50 crore residual band, but this was not confirmed.

## Q6. Data localisation and cross-border transfer — DPDP and health-sector-specific rules

### Takeaway
DPDP uses a **negative-list** model: transfer abroad is permitted by default and restricted only to countries the Central Government notifies; **no country has been named**. The only localisation hook is Rule 13(4), which applies to **Significant Data Fiduciaries only** and awaits a government order specifying which data classes are covered. The real health-data localisation risk comes from outside DPDP — the ABDM/NHA Health Data Management Policy's 2022 revised draft states personal data shall not be stored beyond India's geographical boundaries — but the final in-force status of that clause could not be confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- **s.16 is a negative list:** transfer is allowed except to countries the Central Government restricts by notification — [PRS](https://prsindia.org/billtrack/digital-personal-data-protection-bill-2023); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/cross-border-transfer-healthcare-data-reconciling-india-dpdpa-gdpr-542388)
- **s.16(2) preserves stricter sectoral laws** on cross-border transfer — i.e. an RBI-style or health-sector localisation rule overrides the permissive DPDP default — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/cross-border-transfer-healthcare-data-reconciling-india-dpdpa-gdpr-542388)
- **Rule 15** implements s.16: transfers are generally permitted, subject to restrictions the Central Government specifies by order — described by one commentator as a "free rein with a short leash" policy — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/cross-border-transfer-healthcare-data-reconciling-india-dpdpa-gdpr-542388)
- **No country has been restricted.** The LiveLaw analysis names no jurisdiction on any restricted list and says only that the government *can* restrict countries posing risk — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/cross-border-transfer-healthcare-data-reconciling-india-dpdpa-gdpr-542388)
- **No whitelist, no mandatory SCCs:** India has neither an adequacy whitelist nor a mandatory standard-contractual-clause requirement, which the LiveLaw author criticises as leaving health profiles exposed when sent to weak-protection jurisdictions — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/cross-border-transfer-healthcare-data-reconciling-india-dpdpa-gdpr-542388)
- **Rule 16** exempts transfers for research, archiving or statistical purposes, subject to the standards in the **Second Schedule** — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/cross-border-transfer-healthcare-data-reconciling-india-dpdpa-gdpr-542388)
- **Rule 13(4) (SDF only):** specified personal data and associated traffic data must not be transferred outside India, as identified by the Central Government on a committee's recommendation — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/); [AMSS](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/)
- MeitY's January 2026 proposal included **notifying cross-border restrictions for SDFs** and bringing Rule 15 into effect immediately — still a proposal as of April 2026 — [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs/); [Mondaq, 15 Apr 2026](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1773554/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs)
- **ABDM/NHA Health Data Management Policy:** the revised policy released for consultation in April 2022 added a data-localisation requirement stating that personal data shall not be stored beyond India's geographical boundaries, **"subject to applicable laws"** — [MediaNama, June 2022](https://www.medianama.com/2022/06/223-new-health-data-management-policy/)
- ABDM's architecture is **federated, not centralised**: patient data resides with facilities and is shared with consent across the network — [HMPI, July 2026](https://hmpi.org/2026/07/09/from-infrastructure-to-impact-operationalizing-indias-ayushman-bharat-digital-mission/)
- The NHA ran a separate consultation on a **health data retention policy** aimed at uniform principles for retention, use, storage and accessibility of health data; it remains a draft — [BioVoice News](https://biovoicenews.com/national-health-authority-invites-comments-on-consultation-paper-on-health-data-retention-policy/); [DataGuidance](https://www.dataguidance.com/news/india-nha-launches-consultation-health-data-retention)
- A 2026 commentary notes that DPDP-era governance creates "legal uncertainty for private health technology firms" around consent-artifact storage, localisation and cross-entity sharing — [HMPI, July 2026](https://hmpi.org/2026/07/09/from-infrastructure-to-impact-operationalizing-indias-ayushman-bharat-digital-mission/)

### Inferences
**MANDATORY today:** nothing. There is no DPDP localisation duty on a non-SDF app, and no restricted-country list exists. Hosting a medication app's data on a foreign cloud region is lawful under DPDP as it stands.

**Where the real constraint comes from:**
1. **If the app integrates with ABDM** (ABHA number, Health Information Provider/User role, consent-manager participation), the ABDM Health Data Management Policy and its localisation clause bind you contractually/by policy even though DPDP does not. **Any ABDM integration should be treated as pulling in an India-storage requirement.** s.16(2) expressly preserves such stricter sectoral rules.
2. **If the app is ever notified as an SDF**, Rule 13(4) can impose localisation on specified classes by order, with no transition period guaranteed.

**Engineering recommendation (good practice, not mandatory):** host primary personal data in an India region from day one. The cost of choosing `ap-south-1` now is near zero; the cost of migrating a live health dataset after an ABDM integration or an SDF notification is not. Keep the data-residency decision configurable and documented rather than hard-coded across services. Avoid routing health data to third-party processors in jurisdictions you cannot change quickly (analytics SDKs are the usual offender — a crash reporter that ships a stack trace containing a medication name is an unplanned cross-border transfer of health data).

### Gaps
- **Could not confirm the final, in-force text of the ABDM/HDM Policy localisation clause.** The only citation found is the 2022 revised *draft*. Whether it was finalised in that form, and whether DPDP has superseded it, is unresolved and matters a great deal if ABDM integration is planned. This should be checked directly against abdm.gov.in / nha.gov.in.
- No source found on EHR Standards for India (2016) data-storage requirements, or on whether the NHA retention consultation has been finalised.
- No source confirming whether any s.16 country restriction has been notified between April and October 2026.
- RBI-style localisation (payment data must be stored only in India) was not researched; it would apply only if the app processes payments, and is out of the health-data scope.

## Q7. Consent Manager provisions — what they require and whether the regime is operational

### Takeaway
Consent Managers are registered intermediaries that let a data principal give, manage, review and withdraw consent through a single interoperable platform. Registration conditions are demanding (Indian-incorporated company, ₹2 crore net worth, certified platform, 7-year consent records, fiduciary duty to the data principal). Rule 4 commences around **13 November 2026 — roughly three weeks from today** — but **no Consent Manager has been registered**, and the Board that must register them has no members, so the regime is not operational.

### Cited Findings
- **Registration conditions (Rule 4, First Schedule Part A):** must be a company incorporated in India; demonstrate technical, operational and financial capacity; hold a **minimum net worth of ₹2 crore**; have a reputation for fairness and integrity; and obtain independent certification of an interoperable platform that lets data principals manage consent — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/); [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/)
- Consent Managers act in a **fiduciary capacity toward Data Principals**, must avoid conflicts of interest, and must retain consent records for **seven years** — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/)
- Process: an eligible applicant applies to the Board; the Board may inquire, require additional information, verify compliance, then register or reject, and publishes details of approved Consent Managers — [search synthesis; see](https://www.medianama.com/2025/11/223-explained-obligations-role-requirements-consent-managers-dpdp-rules-2025/) and [King Stubb & Kasiva](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/dpdp-consent-manager-framework-india/)
- Consent Managers must also publish rights-request channels and resolve grievances within 90 days (same Rule 14 duties as fiduciaries) — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/); [AMSS](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/)
- Commencement: Rule 4 and the corresponding s.6(9) and s.27(1)(d) take effect one year after notification — ~13 November 2026 — [AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones/); [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/featured-content/building-indias-digital-privacy-framework-an-overview-of-the-dpdp-rules-2025/)
- MeitY released a **Business Requirement Document on Consent Management Systems** under the DPDP Act in June 2025 — [AZB, 19 June 2025](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/meity-releases-business-requirement-document-on-consent-management-systems-under-the-dpdp-act-2023/)
- **Status:** a miniOrange FAQ states there are currently no registered Consent Managers available for certified integration; no MeitY or Board announcement of a registration portal or published list was found — [miniOrange MeitY consent manager integration FAQ (undated)](https://faq.miniorange.com/knowledgebase/meity-consent-manager-integration/)

### Inferences
- **Using a Consent Manager is an option for the data principal, not an obligation on the Data Fiduciary.** The DPDP architecture is that a data principal *may* give, manage and withdraw consent *through* a Consent Manager; nothing found suggests a small app must onboard one. A medication app is not going to register *as* a Consent Manager (₹2 crore net worth, fiduciary duty, certified interoperable platform — plainly aimed at infrastructure players).
- The practical engineering consequence is forward-compatibility, not immediate work: the consent record model should be able to represent consent that **arrived from, and can be revoked by, an external Consent Manager**. That means consent records need a `source` (direct / consent-manager-id) and a machine-readable consent artefact, and withdrawal must be able to arrive over an API, not only from a UI tap. Designing consent as a UI-only flow will require a rewrite later.
- The ABDM consent-manager model (HIU/HIP consent artefacts) and the DPDP Consent Manager model are distinct but structurally similar; an app planning ABDM integration gets partial alignment for free.
- Since the Board cannot register anyone until members are appointed, the 13 November 2026 date will almost certainly pass with an empty register. Do not plan any dependency on a registered Consent Manager existing.

### Gaps
- Whether a Data Fiduciary will be *required* to accept consent/withdrawal signals from any registered Consent Manager (i.e. mandatory interoperability) is not clearly resolved by the sources found; s.6(9)/Rule 4 text should be read directly.
- No published technical specification/API contract for the Consent Manager interface was located beyond the existence of MeitY's June 2025 Business Requirement Document.
- The miniOrange "no registered Consent Managers" source is undated and low-authority; it is consistent with the Board having no members, but it is weak evidence on its own.

## Q8. Other Indian rules that bite on a health app — telemedicine, Drugs & Cosmetics / e-pharmacy, Clinical Establishments Act, CDSCO SaMD

### Takeaway
CDSCO issued a **final Guidance Document on Medical Device Software on 21 July 2026** (Doc No. CDSCO/MD/GD/MDSW/01/2026) applying a **function-based, intended-purpose test** under the Medical Device Rules, 2017: software is a regulated device if it is intended for a medical purpose, regardless of whether it is a phone app, cloud service or COTS product. Telemedicine Practice Guidelines (2020) bind registered medical practitioners, not app publishers, unless the app enables consultations. E-pharmacy remains governed only by 2018 draft rules that were never notified. The Clinical Establishments Act does not reach a pure software product.

### Cited Findings
**CDSCO / Software as a Medical Device**
- Final guidance issued by CDSCO (Medical Devices Division) by circular dated **21 July 2026**, Circular F. No. MED-16028/2/2025-eoffice; guidance **Doc No. CDSCO/MD/GD/MDSW/01/2026** — [Nishith Desai Associates, "From Code to Compliance"](https://nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/pharma-healthcare-update/from-code-to-compliance-decoding-indias-medical-device-software-guidance-15623)
- CDSCO states the guidance reflects current practice under MDR 2017 and is **not a new regulation** — [PureGlobal](https://www.pureglobal.com/news/india-cdsco-medical-device-software-guidance-2026)
- **Test:** MDSW is software "intended for a medical purpose", standalone or as part of / driving a hardware device. "Medical purpose" covers diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, treatment or alleviation of any disease or disorder, support/sustaining of life, disinfection of devices and control of conception; the guidance extends it to **mitigation, prediction and alleviation of disease or pathological conditions** — [Nishith Desai](https://nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/pharma-healthcare-update/from-code-to-compliance-decoding-indias-medical-device-software-guidance-15623)
- The test is **function-based, not product-based**: software with one additional function serving a medical purpose can be a medical device even if its other functions are excluded — [Nishith Desai](https://nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/pharma-healthcare-update/from-code-to-compliance-decoding-indias-medical-device-software-guidance-15623); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/code-compliance-decoding-indias-medical-device-software-guidance)
- **Exclusions:** software solely for general wellness, fitness, education or training without a medical purpose; software limited to data transfer, storage, archiving, formatting or communication; and certain hospital/clinical information systems, general-purpose communication systems and laboratory information systems where their functions do not independently serve a medical purpose — [Nishith Desai](https://nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/pharma-healthcare-update/from-code-to-compliance-decoding-indias-medical-device-software-guidance-15623)
- General-wellness software may aim to improve general health but **cannot refer to diseases, disorders or pathological conditions**; a "wellness" label does not protect software that performs diagnostic functions — [MedTech Spectrum](https://www.medtechspectrum.com/medicaldevice-softwareindia); [PureGlobal](https://www.pureglobal.com/news/india-cdsco-medical-device-software-guidance-2026)
- **Functions that would make an app a device:** image analysis as a diagnostic aid; quantifying physiological parameters for clinical decision-making; real-time patient monitoring; decision-support; driving a device; providing a definitive diagnosis or treatment recommendation; AI/ML software with a medical purpose — [Nishith Desai](https://nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/pharma-healthcare-update/from-code-to-compliance-decoding-indias-medical-device-software-guidance-15623); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/code-compliance-decoding-indias-medical-device-software-guidance)
- **Classification:** Classes A (low), B (low–moderate), C (moderate–high), D (high) per the MDR First Schedule, based on intended use. Software driving hardware takes that hardware's class. Standalone MDSW uses a matrix weighing the significance of the information for healthcare decisions against the situation (critical / serious / non-serious) — [Nishith Desai](https://nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/pharma-healthcare-update/from-code-to-compliance-decoding-indias-medical-device-software-guidance-15623)
- **Obligations if classified:** application to State/UT Licensing Authority; QMS undertaking or notarised QMS certificate; conformity with BIS, or ISO/IEC/pharmacopoeial standards where no BIS standard is notified; technical documentation (intended use, architecture, risk management, V&V, cybersecurity, clinical evaluation, lifecycle); for AI-enabled MDSW additional documentation on training/validation datasets, performance across populations, bias, explainability and algorithm change protocols; post-market surveillance with records of updates and corrective actions — [Nishith Desai](https://nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/pharma-healthcare-update/from-code-to-compliance-decoding-indias-medical-device-software-guidance-15623)
- The guidance does **not** mention medication reminder, adherence or dosage-calculation software as a named category — [Nishith Desai](https://nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/pharma-healthcare-update/from-code-to-compliance-decoding-indias-medical-device-software-guidance-15623)
- A pre-2026 academic view held that standalone health and wellness apps were not regulated by CDSCO; this predates the July 2026 guidance — [BJPsych International, Cambridge](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/bjpsych-international/article/mental-health-apps-in-india-regulatory-landscape-and-future-directions/4B61B12BA5F14790CEC6214D2FAAD52E)

**Telemedicine**
- The Telemedicine Practice Guidelines date from **2020** and were issued under the Indian Medical Council professional-conduct regulations. They limit telemedicine consultations to **registered medical practitioners**, who may use text, video or audio and must verify patient identity — [IBA](https://www.ibanet.org/article/15234243-b534-45f6-b303-4919cfe9a2dc); [Bhatt & Joshi Associates](https://bhattandjoshiassociates.com/regulation-of-online-pharmacies-and-telemedicine/)
- A doctor may send a prescription directly to a pharmacy after obtaining patient consent; **Schedule X drugs and narcotic/psychotropic drugs cannot be prescribed** after a teleconsultation — [Bhatt & Joshi Associates](https://bhattandjoshiassociates.com/regulation-of-online-pharmacies-and-telemedicine/)

**E-pharmacy / Drugs and Cosmetics Act**
- There is no specific legislation regulating e-pharmacy, apart from the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare's **Draft Rules to Regulate the Sale of Drugs through E-pharmacy, 2018**, which would require every e-pharmacy to register with CDSCO — [Bhatt & Joshi Associates](https://bhattandjoshiassociates.com/regulation-of-online-pharmacies-and-telemedicine/); [Law.asia, India healthtech regulations](https://law.asia/india-healthtech-regulations/)

### Inferences
**Does a medication tracking/reminder app become a medical device in India?**
On the function-based test, a pure reminder-and-log app is most defensibly **outside** the MDSW definition: it stores and displays user-entered data and issues time-based notifications. Those functions map to the express exclusion for software "limited to data transfer, storage, archiving, formatting, communication". There is no diagnosis, no physiological quantification, no decision support, no real-time monitoring.

But the margin is thin, because the guidance's "medical purpose" list explicitly includes **monitoring, prevention, mitigation, prediction and alleviation** of disease. Several very ordinary medication-app features land on the wrong side of that line:
- **Drug–drug interaction checking** → decision support with a medical purpose. Almost certainly a device function.
- **Dose calculation** (e.g. weight-based or renal-adjusted dosing) → treatment-related decision support. Device function.
- **Adherence scoring that produces a clinical recommendation** ("your control is poor, increase your dose / contact your doctor") → treatment recommendation. Device function.
- **Interpreting user-entered vitals** (BP, glucose) and flagging them as abnormal → quantifying physiological parameters for clinical decision-making. Device function.
- **Symptom checking / triage** → diagnosis. Device function.
- **Predicting deterioration or exacerbation from adherence patterns** → prediction of a pathological condition. Device function, and if ML-based, carries the AI documentation burden.
- A **passive adherence report shared with a clinician** is the grey zone: arguably mere data communication, but "real-time patient monitoring" is listed as a device function, so a live clinician-facing alerting dashboard is materially riskier than an exportable PDF the patient hands over.

**What is required to NOT be classified as a medical device — concrete claim discipline:**
1. State an **intended use** explicitly in-app, in the store listing and in the T&Cs, and make it non-medical: an organiser/record-keeping and reminder tool for medicines the user has already been prescribed. Intended use is the hinge of the whole test, and CDSCO reads it from your own claims.
2. **Never name a disease, disorder or pathological condition** in a claim. Wellness software "cannot refer to diseases, disorders, or pathological conditions". "Helps you remember your medicines" is safe; "helps control your diabetes" is not. This rules out condition-specific marketing copy, condition-based onboarding funnels, and condition landing pages.
3. **No claims using the regulated verbs**: diagnose, treat, prevent, monitor, mitigate, predict, alleviate, cure, manage [a condition]. Avoid "clinically proven", "medical-grade", "doctor-recommended" as efficacy claims.
4. **No output that a clinician or patient would act on as a clinical decision.** No interaction warnings, no dose suggestions, no "abnormal" flags, no triage. If a drug database is included at all, ship it as reference information attributed to its source, not as a personalised alert.
5. **Carry a conspicuous disclaimer** that the app does not provide medical advice and is not a substitute for a healthcare professional — necessary but not sufficient; a disclaimer does not rescue a device-like function, because the test is function-based and a wellness label "alone does not protect software that performs diagnostic functions".
6. **Treat it as a design gate, not a copy review.** Because the test is function-based and one additional medical-purpose function taints the product, any new feature that computes a clinical conclusion should go through a check before it ships. The guidance's own framing — adding image analysis or physiological quantification to an otherwise excluded app makes it a device — is the clearest statement of this.

**Telemedicine, e-pharmacy, Clinical Establishments:**
- Telemedicine Practice Guidelines bind the **doctor**, not the software. They become relevant only if the app facilitates consultations; then identity verification, consent, and the Schedule X / NDPS prescribing prohibition all apply to the practitioner using it, and the app must not design flows that induce non-compliant prescribing.
- The Drugs and Cosmetics Act and the 2018 draft e-pharmacy rules bite only if the app sells, facilitates the sale of, or dispenses medicines. A reminder app that merely records what the user takes does **not** touch this. Adding a "reorder this medicine" button does, and should be treated as a separate regulatory project.
- The Clinical Establishments (Registration and Regulation) Act, 2010 applies to physical clinical establishments, not to a software publisher. No source in this research addressed software; see Gaps.

**NOT legally required (good practice):** writing an intended-use statement and a documented non-device rationale, versioned, and re-reviewed at each release. Not mandatory, but it is the single cheapest artefact to produce and the most useful if CDSCO or a hospital partner ever asks.

### Gaps
- The CDSCO guidance's treatment of **medication reminder / adherence / dosage software specifically is absent** from the secondary sources. The classification above is inference from the general test, not a cited CDSCO position. The official PDF (CDSCO/MD/GD/MDSW/01/2026) should be read directly — this is the single highest-value follow-up in this research.
- The standalone-MDSW classification matrix (significance of information × critical/serious/non-serious situation) was not reproduced by any source, so no class could be assigned to any candidate feature.
- Did not verify whether the 2018 e-pharmacy draft rules have been notified at any point up to 2026; the sources are of uncertain date. A primary check on cdsco.gov.in is needed before any dispensing/reorder feature is scoped.
- Found no source on whether the Clinical Establishments Act or any state analogue has been extended to digital health providers; this was not confirmed either way.
- The Telemedicine Practice Guidelines 2020 have reportedly been affected by the replacement of the Medical Council of India by the National Medical Commission; no source found confirming their current status or any 2026 revision.
